They Actually Sprouted

The Double Purple Brugmansia Seeds Came Up

Seed planted 7/25. First proof the double purple came true.

Seems like a good week for new sprouts all around. This newsletter's picked up a bunch of new faces lately, and out in the beds, the garden's doing the same.

Michael from LRH2 gifted me a double purple brugmansia plant over a year ago. It's bloomed multiple times since, and I finally gathered a seed pod this round. Doubles don't always cooperate from seed, so I wasn't expecting much. Two of eight have sprouted so far.

Too early to know what these turn into, but if they hold true, it's a genuinely rare bloom for a San Antonio yard. I'll keep you posted, and if they turn out right, expect some at the LRH stands.

Worth saying plainly: brugmansia is beautiful and it's poisonous, every part of it. Fine in a yard where nobody's eating the leaves. Keep it away from small kids and pets that put things in their mouths.
 
Garden Safety

Texas Caterpillars: No ID, No Touchy

Found this fuzzy little dude in the garden this week. Several Texas caterpillars carry venomous spines under fuzzy hair that looks completely harmless, and by the time you realize which kind you're holding, it's already too late. You can't tell them apart by softness or color alone. Keep it simple: no ID, no touchy.

Known Offenders in Texas

Asp / puss caterpillar — teardrop-shaped, tan and fuzzy, looks like a toupee. AgriLife calls it the most painful sting in the state, bad enough for a hospital visit.
Buck moth — dark, spiny, moves in groups on oak trees. Small but it hits hard.
Saddleback — the bright green "saddle" on its back is a warning label, not decoration.
Io moth — pale green, almost pretty, with rows of spines that mean business.
Hickory tussock — white and black, common in fall. Milder than the others, but the hairs still irritate skin.

Most of these show up on trees and shrubs more than vegetable beds, but San Antonio yards back up to both. Worth teaching kids the rule early: fuzzy or spiny, hands stay off until an adult knows what it is.

If someone does get stung:

1. Press tape over the area and peel it off to pull out any leftover spines.

2. Wash with soap and water, then apply an ice pack.

3. An oral antihistamine helps with itching. Pain usually eases within an hour. If the reaction is severe, get it looked at.

Making It Official

I Just Joined Bexar County Master Gardener Crew 69

One of the recent meetups.

Made it official, and I start this month. Training with the elite, and getting closer with SABG (San Antonio Botanical Garden) in the process. Here's what that actually opens up, and the connections that come with it, in case you want in too.

Master Gardener Training

Runs through Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Bexar County. Certified volunteers build the Little Buckaroo Barn for the San Antonio Rodeo, staff plant clinics at events like the Monarch Festival and Earth Day, and work the Children's Vegetable Garden at SABG, the same program sending kids there this month.

SABG Volunteering

No certification required. Horticulture, education, guest experience, corporate team days. Apply at sabgtx.org/volunteer.

GROW Workshop

Dementia-friendly gardening at SABG, run with UT Health San Antonio's Caring for the Caregiver program. Free, weekly during the season, open to people living with dementia and their caregivers. I volunteer with this one regularly.
