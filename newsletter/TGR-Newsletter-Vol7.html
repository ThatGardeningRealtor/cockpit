Choosing an Agent

The Difference Between an Agent Who Sells Here and One Who Lives Here

Any agent can unlock a door. Not every agent knows why some streets in Helotes back up to the Balcones Escarpment and carry drainage quirks, or why a family fiercely loyal to NEISD won't even look at a home two blocks outside the line. That kind of knowledge doesn't come from a script. It comes from being planted here.

A community-first agent shows up at the same places you do. They know which HEB is worth the extra traffic, which neighborhoods are quietly growing before the rest of the city notices, and which inspector has actually seen what San Antonio's clay soils do to a foundation over time. Those relationships aren't a nice bonus. They're protection for you at every stage of the deal.

Two Questions Worth Asking Before You Sign Anything

"How many transactions have you closed in this specific area?" An agent can be highly productive citywide and still have zero deals in the zip code you actually care about. If you're set on 78209 or 78108, ask for the number. A good agent shows you the receipts without hesitating.

"Can you walk me through the Option Period and how you handle Texas-specific disclosures?" Texas contracts are agent-prepared, not attorney-drafted at the table. Your agent's contract competency directly affects your legal protection, so this isn't a formality question. It's the one that tells you whether they actually know the paperwork or just move it along.

Community-first isn't a slogan on a business card. It's showing up, asking better questions, and sticking around long after closing day.

For Buyers

Two Red Flags Worth Walking Away From

Be cautious of any agent who pressures you to waive your Option Period. It's a uniquely Texas tool, usually 5 to 10 days, giving you the unrestricted right to back out for any reason. That's a risk that should be yours to take knowingly, never quietly absorbed into a contract without discussion.

Also watch for an agent who can't name three comparable sales in your neighborhood from the last 90 days without looking them up on the spot. That's a sign they don't actively work the area you're trying to buy in.

Seller Tip of the Week

Ask Your Agent for Their List-to-Sale Ratio

This one number tells you whether an agent prices homes accurately or over-promises just to win the listing. A strong ratio in this market is 98 to 101%. If an agent's average is consistently running below 96%, their pricing strategy may be costing you money at closing, not saving it.
