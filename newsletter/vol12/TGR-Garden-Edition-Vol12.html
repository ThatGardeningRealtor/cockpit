Now Is the Time to Trim Your Oaks

A watercolor I painted with the Wednesday dementia friendly gardening group at the San Antonio Botanical Garden.

Our trees class was one of my favorites so far. Trees take years to grow up, they don't get to move, and what happens to one can end up affecting the ones down the street. Nowhere is that clearer than with oak wilt.

Oak wilt is a deadly fungal disease that clogs the system an oak uses to move water. All oaks can get it, some more than others, and there's no known cure. It spreads two ways. Above ground, fungal mats form under the bark of infected red oaks, mostly in spring, and their fruity smell draws in tiny sap feeding beetles that carry the spores to fresh cuts on healthy oaks. Below ground, it moves through connected roots, which is how it can take out a whole group of live oaks.

Our instructor made a point that stuck with me: a tree's roots aren't a mirror image of its canopy. They run shallow and spread out wide, out searching for water. So here's what we can all do:

 

Paint every cut, right away
Any paint works. Our instructor's tip was the cheapest can of flat black you can find. San Antonio's oak wilt ordinance says cuts and wounds on oaks have to be painted within 30 minutes.

Trim in late fall or winter
Skip trimming oaks from February through June, when the beetles and fungal mats are most active. The Texas A&M Forest Service says late fall is the best time to prune oaks, which makes right now the window.

Don't stack oak firewood
Fungal mats can form on fresh firewood cut from diseased red oaks, so that wood should be chipped or buried, not stacked by the back fence.
 
 

Before you hire

Who Gets to Climb Your Tree?

This time of year the tree trimmers start knocking on doors. In San Antonio, anyone you pay to prune, treat, or remove trees has to hold a city Tree Maintenance License. To get one, they need at least $300,000 in general liability insurance and a class that covers oak wilt, and they agree to paint every oak wound they make. So before anybody climbs your tree, ask to see the license and proof of insurance. You can also look them up on the City's Find a Contractor page.

Doing it yourself? If you live in a finished home, you don't need a city permit to prune or remove trees on your own property. Our instructor's rule of thumb still applies for bigger projects around the house, though: assume you need a permit until somebody tells you otherwise.

And that gray fuzz in so many San Antonio trees? Ball moss isn't a parasite. It's an epiphyte in the bromeliad family, the same family as the pineapple, and it pulls what it needs from the air. It'll hang on to a power line just as happily as a branch. It usually shows up on dead or dying limbs deep inside a shady canopy, so think of it as a sign there's dead wood to clean out, not the thing hurting your tree.

 

Try This Today

Walk your yard this weekend and look over your oaks. Fresh cuts or broken limbs? Paint them today. Need real trimming done? Get it on the calendar before February.

Fall Garden

The Cool Season Crew Moved In

The raised beds on Wednesday were a good reminder that fall gardening pays you back a little at a time. Long, crinkled leaves on the dinosaur kale, marigolds between the plants, and a broccoli head starting to fill out.

Lacinato kale, also called dinosaur kale, with marigolds between the plants.

A broccoli head taking shape, with the scarecrow still on duty.

 

Kale: start with the outside leaves
Those long outer leaves are the ones to pick first. Leave the center growing so the plant can keep sending out fresh greens.

Broccoli: watch the head, not the calendar
Let the forming head fill out, then cut it while it is firm and the little florets are still tight and green, before any yellow shows. Leave the plant in place for smaller side shoots later.

Source: Texas A&M AgriLife Extension vegetable harvesting guides.

What Happens to the Trees When New Homes Go Up?

New slabs on freshly graded lots. Notice what's missing?

This one came straight out of tree class. When land gets developed in San Antonio, the builder can't just scrape it clean and walk away. The City's tree ordinance counts trees 6 inches across or bigger as significant and 24 inches or bigger as heritage trees. Developers have to survey them, then preserve trees, plant new ones, or pay into the City's Tree Mitigation Fund, and new home lots get at least two newly planted trees.

Here's my favorite part: the free trees at the City's adoption events, like Día del Árbol next Saturday, are paid for with money from that mitigation fund. Our instructor also pointed out that the trees that do get saved usually end up around the outside edges of a subdivision, because the inside gets graded for streets and drainage. If you're shopping new construction, that's worth knowing:

 

Ask about the trees on your lot
Which ones were kept, which ones were planted, and what kind they are, so you know what you are taking care of.

Watch the grading
Grading that sends water toward the foundation is one of the common problems inspectors find in new San Antonio builds. That is one reason I always recommend a pre drywall and a final inspection.

Call me before the model home
Most builders want your agent registered on your very first visit to the community, so give me a call before you walk in.

Right Plant, Right Place

Pick a Tree That Will Grow Up Here

If you're grabbing a free tree on November 14, or buying one this winter, here's what stuck with me from our class outing to another garden. The first rule was blunt: fast growing trees tend to be fast dying trees. The second was simple: buy from a reputable source, and pick something that can actually reach maturity here. A few that came up:

 

Mexican white oak
Also sold as Monterrey oak. It is a Texas Superstar shade tree that handles heat, wind, and dry spells, stays mostly green through our mild winters, and has leaves that change shape from tree to tree. That is where its Latin name, polymorpha, comes from.

Lacey oak
A Hill Country native from the limestone of the Edwards Plateau, with blue gray leaves that stand out from everything around it. It grows slowly to around 40 feet.

Texas or Mexican redbud
Both handle our alkaline soil. The Mexican variety has smaller, glossy, wavy leaves, takes heat and drought even better, and stays around 10 to 15 feet.

One more nice thing about those two oaks: they're in the white oak group. The fungal mats that start new oak wilt spots form on infected red oaks, not on white oaks or live oaks.

 

Try This Today

Before you pick up a tree, stand where you want to plant it and look up. Power lines, the roofline, and the foundation all matter. Picture the tree at full size, not the size it is in the bucket. Call 811 before you dig, and make the hole two to three times as wide as the root ball.
 

Sources: Master Gardener class outing, October 22, 2026, and trees class, October 15, 2026; Texas A&M AgriLife Texas Superstar; Texas Tree Selector; Native Plant Society of Texas; Texas A&M Forest Service.

Oxalis, also called purple shamrock, folds its leaves up at night like it's calling it a day. It's also one of my favorite snacks in the garden. I enjoy a leaf now and then: crisp, fresh, and a little sour.

Those glossy purple pods are hyacinth beans. The planting also has passion flower vines and lima beans. Pretty on a fence, but the dried beans aren't safe to eat raw.
