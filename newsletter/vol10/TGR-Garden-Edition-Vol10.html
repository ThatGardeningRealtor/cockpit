Your Lawn Runs on Sunshine

My rain gauge after our last good storm, about an inch and a half.

Dr. Emanuel Chavarria with Texas A&M AgriLife taught our turfgrass class, and the line I keep repeating is this one: grass doesn't eat fertilizer. Its real food is sunlight. Fertilizer is more like vitamins. So the first question for any lawn is how much sun it actually gets.

 

St. Augustine
Handles some shade better than Bermuda and roots deep. Watch out for cold snaps and chinch bugs. Fun fact: there's no St. Augustine seed for sale, so it always starts from sod or plugs.

Bermuda
Tough, fast, and handles heat, drought, and foot traffic. It wants more than 8 hours of sun, or it thins out and fades in the shade.

Zoysia
Handles shade, drought, and cold, and doesn't need much fertilizer. Its stiff blades dull a mower fast, so keep that blade sharp.

Buffalograss
A Texas native that needs about as little water and care as any lawn grass gets. It does better out west than in wetter East Texas.

Mowing is where most of us get in trouble. Follow the one third rule: never cut off more than a third of the blade at once. Those blades are the solar panels, and scalping them leaves the grass with an empty kitchen.

 

Try This Today

Do the catch can test. Set out a few empty tuna or cat food cans around the yard, run your sprinklers for 5 minutes, and measure the water in each one. Average them and multiply by 12 to see how many inches you put down in an hour. Then check WaterMyYard.org for how much your lawn actually needs this week.
 
 
Planting Season

Plant a Tree That Outlives Your Mortgage

Our young Celeste fig is already setting fruit. Fall is when trees like this one get their roots down.

October through April is tree planting season here, and the City of San Antonio's urban forestry team walked us through how to do it right. Here's the short version:

 

Call 811 first
Before you dig, call 811 so the utility lines get marked. It's free.

Go wide, not deep
Dig the hole 2 to 3 times as wide as the root ball, but no deeper. Roots spread out sideways looking for water, way past the edge of the branches.

Water slow
A 5 gallon bucket with a small hole in the bottom lets water soak in slowly instead of running off.

Paint every oak cut
Oak wilt spreads by a little sap beetle drawn to fresh wounds. Paint any cut on an oak right away (cheap flat black spray paint works) and save big oak pruning for winter.

What to plant? From this week's class: Lacey oak, Mexican white oak, Texas or Mexican redbud, and Barbados cherry if you want something compact the birds will love. The warning I wrote down twice: fast growing trees are often fast dying trees. Pick one that can actually reach maturity here.

And that gray, fuzzy stuff hanging in your oaks? That's ball moss. It's not a parasite. It's a bromeliad, kin to pineapples, and it pulls everything it needs from the air. It will grow on a power line just as happily as a branch. If a tree is loaded with it, that's usually a sign of shady, dead, or stressed limbs, so focus on the tree's health and clear out the dead wood.

Real Estate Corner

What a Yard Tells You Before You Buy

A mature shade tree like this one can change how a whole house lives.

When I walk a property with someone, I look at the house, sure. But I'm also reading the yard, because the yard tells you what living there will actually be like. Here's what I check:

 

Where the sun goes
Full sun is 6 or more hours of direct light, part sun is 3 to 6, and deep shade is under 3. A backyard that's shady at 2 PM won't grow tomatoes or Bermuda. If you can, visit at different times of day.

Big trees
Shade trees take decades to grow, so look at them closely. Is the canopy full? Any dead limbs, or ball moss piling up on stressed wood? If there are oaks, ask whether oak wilt has been in the area.

The tree rules
San Antonio protects trees by trunk size: most are significant at 6 inches across and heritage at 24 inches, measured at chest height. If you're planning to build, add on, or clear, check the City's tree rules first.

The lawn and the water
What kind of grass is it, and is there a sprinkler system? A catch can test on a showing day tells you a lot.

And for everyone who already owns a home here, property tax bills are landing in mailboxes now. In Bexar County, the last day to pay without penalty is normally January 31, and since that's a Sunday this time, it rolls to Monday, February 1, 2027. Bexar also offers payment plans, so check your options early.

While you've got the bill in hand, make sure your homestead exemption shows up on it. Texas school districts are required to give a $140,000 homestead exemption, and if you bought this year and haven't filed, you can generally still file late (up to two years after the deadline). BCAD has the form and can confirm what you qualify for.

Sources: City of San Antonio Tree Preservation Ordinance and tree ordinance FAQ; Bexar County Tax Assessor Collector tax calendar; Bexar Appraisal District exemption overview and Form 50 114. This is general info, not tax advice.

Right Plant, Right Place

Do the Sun Walk This Weekend

Cosmos love full sun, and so do the bees. Right plant, right place.

Lynn Cox taught our perennials class, and her homework is something anyone can do. On a sunny day, check your yard every hour or so from 10 AM to 5 PM and jot down where the light lands. That's your real map for what to plant where. Do it again in spring, because the sun angle changes and leaves come and go.

Turk's cap is a good example. In full sun it blooms like crazy but droops in the afternoon heat. In shade the leaves look great but the flowers slow down. Lynn's answer: morning sun, afternoon shade.

Two more tips from her: group plants that need the same water (that's called hydrozoning), and plan for the mature size. Plants crammed too close end up fighting each other and stay stunted.
 
Late October Checklist

Before the First Cold Snap

 

Overseed with the right rye
If you want winter green, use perennial ryegrass, not annual rye.

Find a spot for tender plants
Pick where your potted tropicals will go before the first freeze, not the night of.

Raise the mower
One third rule all the way into winter. Taller grass holds up better.

Get trees in the ground
The roots settle in all winter while the tops rest.

Check your lantana
Silvery, speckled leaves with dark spots underneath usually mean lace bugs.

Sources: Bexar County Master Gardener classes with Texas A&M AgriLife and the City of San Antonio.
